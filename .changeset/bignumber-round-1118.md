`BigNumber` `add`, `subtract` and `multiply` round the result to `config.precision` with the configured rounding mode, as decimal.js and mathjs do. Before, the square of a 64-digit value had up to 128 digits, and `add(bignumber('1'), bignumber('1e-30'))` at precision 20 gave `1.000000000000000000000000000001` (now `1`). A repeated product no longer grows without bound: a stage value inside a BigNumber `solveODE` step had 515 digits. An integer `pow` runs its products with guard digits and rounds once. Constructors, `negate` and `abs` keep every digit. Integer arithmetic beyond the precision loses digits, as in mathjs and decimal.js: `add(bignumber('1e30'), bignumber('1'))` at precision 20 gives 10^30 (the 1 is lost), and `factorial(bignumber(60))` at precision 64 has 64 significant digits. `mod` is exact and does not round, so `gcd` and the floored factory `mod` stay exact for integers longer than the precision. `xgcd`, `invmod` and `isPrime` raise the precision for their integer steps. `lcm`, `combinations` of the factory layer (with `composition` and `catalan`), `bellNumbers`, `factorial` and `gamma` of an integer run their steps with guard digits and round the result one time: a result that fits the precision is exact. `log1p`, `asinh`, `acosh`, `atanh` and `acos` keep a small argument and a result near 0 (`log1p(bignumber('1e-40'))` is 1e-40, not 0; `asinh` of a large negative value is finite). The `engineering` notation of `format` keeps every digit of a value. `compare` and `mod` of two values with the same large exponent build no long string. The release is a patch because the documented contract is `config.precision`. Results of more than `precision` digits were a defect, not a feature (Fixes #1118).

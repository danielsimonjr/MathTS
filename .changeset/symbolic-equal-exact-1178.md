`symbolicEqual` is `true` for two expressions that are the same polynomial with exact rational coefficients (Fixes #1178; this also fixes item 3 of #1014). `symbolicEqual` asked only whether `simplify` reduces the difference of the two sides to 0. An irrational constant beside a fraction folds to a decimal, so an exact zero left a rounding residue, and `simplify` does not expand `(x+1)^2`. The new module `cas/exact-identity.ts` reads both sides as polynomials over their variables and their function calls (`sqrt(2)` is one unknown) and compares them with integer arithmetic. These pairs were `false` and are now `true`: `1/3+sqrt(2)-sqrt(2)` and `1/3`; `sqrt(2)/3+1/3` and `(sqrt(2)+1)/3`; `(x+1/3)*sqrt(3)` and `sqrt(3)*x+sqrt(3)/3`; `(x+1)^2` and `x^2+2*x+1`. mathjs 15.2.0 gives `false` for these pairs, so this is a deliberate difference from mathjs. The exact test runs only when the caller gives no options, so a context such as `{ multiply: { commutative: false } }` keeps its meaning. A `false` still means "not decided": a matrix, a string, a call of `random`, a constant without a finite value (`log(0)`), a degree above 1000, a term count above 10000, and a product of more than 100000 term pairs are all left to `simplify`. A fuzz run of 1500 pairs found no case where the exact test said `true` and a numeric evaluation at three points disagreed. A coefficient of more than 4096 bits is also left to `simplify`: without that limit, `symbolicEqual('(x+1e300)^300', '(x+1e300)^300')` took 12 seconds. (Refs #1178)

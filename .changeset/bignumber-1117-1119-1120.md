BigNumber handling fixes (Fixes #1117, #1119, #1120).

- #1117: a Fraction converts to a BigNumber as numerator / denominator at the configured precision, not through a 16-digit number. `add(fraction(1, 3), bignumber(1))` gave 1.3333333333333333 and now gives 1.333... to 64 digits. `quantileSeq([fraction(1, 3), fraction(2, 3), fraction(1)], bignumber(3))` gave 0.49999999999999995 and 0.8333333333333333 for the outer quantiles and now gives 0.5 and 0.8333... to 64 digits. A BigNumber method that takes a Fraction (`BigNumber.add(fraction)`) converts the same way. This departs from mathjs 15.2.0 on purpose: mathjs throws a TypeError for `add(fraction, bignumber)`; MathTS keeps its implicit conversion and now keeps the digits. `bignumber(fraction)` already matched mathjs.
- #1119: `bignumber()` returns 0, as mathjs. `bignumber(undefined)` throws a TypeError that names `bignumber` (mathjs also throws; the old error was a raw "reading 'isMatrix'"). `bignumber(complex(1, 2))` and `bignumber({})` throw a TypeError, as mathjs (they gave 2.236... and NaN). `bignumber(1, 2)` throws a TypeError ("Too many arguments"), as mathjs (it returned 1). `bignumber('0x1F')`, `'0b101'`, `'0o17'`, a radix fraction (`'0x1.8'`), a binary exponent (`'0x1p3'` is 8) and the word-size form `'0xFFi8'` parse as in mathjs (they threw a SyntaxError), rounded to the configured precision. A word size larger than the value (`'0xFFi999999999'`) gives the value at once. As in mathjs, a word size with a fraction (`'0x1.8i8'`), an upper-case `I` and surrounding spaces throw a SyntaxError. Two departures from mathjs 15.2.0: a sign applies after the word-size conversion (`'-0xFFi8'` is 1; mathjs ignores the sign and gives -1), and the binary exponent must be at most 100000 in magnitude.
- #1120: `solveODE` with `RK23` or `RK45` and a BigNumber `tspan` or `y0` uses the same first-step heuristic as the number path (one shared function). For `y' = -y` on [0, 1], RK23 gave y(1) = 1/3 in 2 steps and now gives 0.3679 (e^-1) in many steps. This departs from mathjs 15.2.0 on purpose: mathjs returns 0.3333333333333334 in 2 steps for the same call. A BigNumber t, y and step size round to the configured precision at each step, so the digits do not grow (`y0 = bignumber('1e2')` did not finish; it now takes the 177 steps of the number path). The first-step norms are scaled by the largest magnitude, so a state near 1e160 (number or BigNumber) no longer gives a NaN first step.

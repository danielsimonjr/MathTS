`besselY(n, x)` returns `-Infinity` when `Y_n(x)` is outside the double range, and it returned `NaN`. `besselY(154, 1)`, `besselY(400, 1)`, `besselY(1000, 100)` and `besselY(400.5, 1)` are `-Infinity`; `besselY(-401, 1)` is `Infinity` (the sign of a negative odd order). Values below the overflow keep their digits: `besselY(151, 1)` is -5.1999e307. The AssemblyScript kernel `bessel_yn_f64` returns the same infinities as the JS scalar. A real order with `nu / x` above about 150 was `NaN` for every `x` (the downward recurrence overflowed): `besselY(400.3, 3)` and `besselY(1000.3, 50)` are `-Infinity`, and `besselJ(300.5, 1)` is `0`. A negative non-integer order reduces `nu` modulo 2 and puts the factor `cos(nu pi)` (for `besselY`) or `-sin(nu pi)` (for `besselJ`) into the upward recurrence of `Y`, so a result that is a double stays finite where `Y_nu` alone overflows: `besselY(-100.5, 1)` is 5.94e-190 (it was -1.8e172), `besselY(-151.5001, 1)` is -2.84e305, `besselY(-152.5, 1)` is 7.63e-315, `besselJ(-152.0001, 1)` is 4.94e306, `besselY(-400.3, 1)` is `-Infinity`, `besselJ(-400.3, 1)` is `Infinity` and `besselY(-400.5, 1)` is `0`. A non-finite `x` stays `NaN`. (Refs #1289)
